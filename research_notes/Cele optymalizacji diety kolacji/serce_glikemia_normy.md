# Measurable dietary criteria for heart health (LDL) and glycaemic control, for scoring dinners

Verification legend: [F] means the figure was read from a fetched page in this session. [K] means the figure is from background knowledge of the primary document, and the URL was not fetched in this session. The report writer should treat [K] items as needing a check against the primary source.

## 1. ESC/EAS dyslipidaemia guidelines (2019 + 2025 focused update): diet criteria for LDL

### Takeaway
The 2019 ESC/EAS lifestyle table remains the current numeric dietary basis. The 2025 focused update (ESC Congress, Aug 2025) changed risk stratification and pharmacotherapy, not the diet numbers. It also explicitly does not recommend supplements without proven LDL-lowering efficacy.

### Cited Findings
- 2019 ESC/EAS dietary targets [F]:
  - Saturated fat <10% of energy, or <7% with hypercholesterolaemia. Replace it with MUFA/PUFA.
  - Avoid trans fat entirely.
  - Dietary cholesterol <300 mg/day.
  - Total fibre 25–40 g/day, including ≥7–13 g soluble fibre.
  - Beta-glucan ≥3 g/day from oats or barley.
  - Plant sterols/stanols ≥2 g/day, ideally with a main meal.
  - Fruit 2–3 servings/day and vegetables 2–3 servings/day.
  - Oily fish 1–2 servings/week. Nuts are recommended, with no amount given.
  - Added sugar at most 10% of energy, alcohol <10 g/day and salt <5 g/day.
  - Source: [Benecol summary of 2019 EAS/ESC guidelines (PDF)](https://evermade-benecol-multisite-website.s3.eu-north-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2021/03/18060759/Summary-of-EAS-ESC-Guidelines.pdf). Caveat: this is an industry summary. Its "healthy diet" box also draws on the 2016 European CVD prevention guidelines.
- Expected size of the LDL-C effect [F]:
  - About 5–10% (++) each for avoiding trans fat, cutting saturated fat, raising fibre, phytosterols at ≥2 g/day and weight loss.
  - Under 5% (+) for cutting dietary cholesterol.
  - Phytosterols alone at 2 g/day lower LDL-C by about 7–10%.
  - Source: [same summary PDF](https://evermade-benecol-multisite-website.s3.eu-north-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2021/03/18060759/Summary-of-EAS-ESC-Guidelines.pdf)
- 2025 focused update [F]:
  - Supplements and vitamins without documented safety and significant LDL-C-lowering efficacy are not recommended.
  - High-dose icosapent ethyl gets a new Class IIa recommendation for high-risk patients with raised triglycerides. This is a drug, not a dietary target.
  - Source: [ESC congress news](https://www.escardio.org/Congresses-Events/ESC-Congress/Congress-news/focused-update-of-the-2019-esc-eas-guidelines-for-the-management-of-dyslipidaemi)
- Secondary summaries describe the 2025 update as adding Lp(a), SCORE2/SCORE2-OP and CAC to risk stratification. They mention no change to diet recommendations [F]. Source: [Metabolicum](https://www.metabolicum.com/news/2025-esc-eas-dyslipidaemia-update)

### Inferences
- Meal-scoring metrics for the "heart/LDL" goal:
  - Saturated fat as % of meal energy: green ≤7%, yellow 7–10%, red >10%.
  - Trans fat: any industrial source is a red flag.
  - Cholesterol per dinner: ≤100 mg is green, about 33% of the 300 mg daily limit. Weight it lightly because its LDL effect is small.
  - Fibre per dinner: ≥8–13 g, i.e. 33% of 25–40 g.
  - Soluble fibre or beta-glucan: give a bonus. Oats, barley and legumes are the main sources.
  - Weekly: oily fish 1–2 times/week. For a vegetarian user, swap this for an ALA source (flax, walnuts) and give partial credit only. Nuts are a positive.
- Show by default for "heart": saturated fat in g and % kcal, fibre g, salt g, sat:unsaturated ratio, oily fish/legume count per week.

### Gaps
- The full text of the 2025 focused update (Eur Heart J) was not read. Confirming the "no diet change" status relies on secondary summaries plus the ESC news page.
- AHA 2021 dietary guidance was not fetched. It is qualitative (pattern-based) in any case [K].

## 2. ADA Standards of Care (2025/2026) Section 5: carbohydrate quality, fibre, GI/GL

### Takeaway
ADA recommends individualised eating patterns with no single ideal macronutrient split. Its numeric anchors are:
- fibre ≥14 g/1000 kcal (more than 35 g/day may help glycaemia further)
- minimal added sugars and refined grains, with water in place of sugary drinks
- sodium <2300 mg/day
- saturated fat in line with general population advice

GL bands per serving are conventional, not ADA-set: low ≤10, medium 11–19, high ≥20.

### Cited Findings
- ADA 2026 [F]:
  - Fibre at 14 g per 1000 kcal, roughly 25–38 g/day. Intakes above 35 g/day, up to 50 g, may give extra glycaemic benefit.
  - Emphasis on eating patterns over single nutrients.
  - Section 5 doi: 10.2337/dc26-S005.
  - Source: [diabetesed.net summary of ADA 2026](https://diabetesed.net/?p=74834)
- ADA 2025 behaviours [F]: vegetables as the base of homemade dishes, herbs and spices instead of salt, and water as the primary drink. Source: [diabetesed.net 2025](https://diabetesed.net/rationale-of-the-week-2025-ada-standards-key-nutrition-behaviors-for-individuals-with-diabetes/)
- ADA Section 5 recommendations [K]:
  - Minimise added sugars and refined grains, and favour non-starchy vegetables, legumes, whole grains and fruit.
  - Sodium <2300 mg/day.
  - Low-carbohydrate and Mediterranean patterns are acceptable options.
  - No routine protein reduction (usual intake is fine; 0.8 g/kg/day in CKD).
  - No benefit from omega-3 supplements, but eat fish and nuts.
  - Fetching the primary Diabetes Care page failed. Source: [Diabetes Care S5 (not retrievable)](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S89/163926/5-Facilitating-Positive-Health-Behaviors-and-Well)
- Glycaemic load per serving [F]: high ≥20, medium 11–19, low ≤10. No daily GL cut-offs are given there. Source: [Wikipedia: Glycemic load](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycemic_load)
- GI categories [K]: low ≤55, medium 56–69, high ≥70 (University of Sydney/ISO 26642 convention). A daily GL under 80 is often called low and over 120 high (Sydney convention). Neither was confirmed in this session.

### Inferences
- Meal-scoring metrics for the "glycaemia" goal:
  - Meal GL: green ≤20, yellow 20–30, red >30. Per-serving bands come from single foods, so a full dinner reasonably gets about twice the single-food band. This mapping is a design choice, not a guideline.
  - Fibre density: ≥14 g/1000 kcal is green, 10–14 yellow, <10 red.
  - Free/added sugar per dinner: ≤5 g green, >10 g red. This assumes a 2000 kcal day with dinner taking 33% of the WHO 5–10% sugar limit.
  - Net carbs and refined grain share: a bonus for whole grains or legumes, a flag for white rice, white pasta or white bread as the main base.
  - Protein ≥20–25 g per dinner as a positive. This is a design inference, not an ADA number.
- Show by default for "glycaemia": carbs g, fibre g and g/1000 kcal, estimated GL, sugars g, protein g.

### Gaps
- The exact 2026 recommendation numbers and evidence grades were not verified from the primary text (fetch failed).
- No authoritative per-meal GL threshold for a mixed meal was found.

## 3. General population limits (WHO, EFSA) and per-dinner/per-week translation

### Takeaway
Core daily anchors:
- salt <5 g (sodium <2 g)
- free sugars <10% of energy, ideally <5%
- saturated fat ≤10% and trans fat ≤1% of energy
- fibre ≥25 g
- fruit and vegetables ≥400 g
- EPA+DHA 250 mg
- potassium 3.5 g

With dinner at about 33% of daily intake, each daily limit converts to a per-dinner share. Weekly items are summed over 7 dinners.

### Cited Findings
- WHO 2023 fats and carbohydrates guideline [F]:
  - Total fat ≤30% of energy, saturated fat ≤10% and trans fat ≤1% (industrial plus ruminant).
  - Naturally occurring fibre ≥25 g/day.
  - Fruit and vegetables ≥400 g/day.
  - Carbohydrates mainly from whole grains, vegetables, fruit and pulses.
  - Source: [WHO news 17-07-2023](https://www.who.int/news/item//17-07-2023-who-updates-guidelines-on-fats-and-carbohydrates)
- WHO 2015 sugars guideline [K]: free sugars <10% of energy (strong recommendation), with a further reduction to <5% (conditional). Source: [WHO Guideline: Sugars intake (2015)](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241549028)
- WHO sodium (2012, reaffirmed 2023 global report) [K]: <2 g sodium/day, about 5 g salt. Potassium ≥3.51 g/day (WHO 2012) [K].
- EFSA [F]:
  - Fibre 25 g/day for adults.
  - Long-chain omega-3 (EPA+DHA) 250 mg/day.
  - Total fat 20–35% of energy.
  - Insufficient evidence to set a sugars upper limit (2010 release).
  - Source: [EFSA press release](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/press/news/nda100326)
- EFSA 2019 [K]: sodium 2.0 g/day is "safe and adequate" for adults. Source: [EFSA sodium opinion PMC7009309](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7009309), see also [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2019/09/11/EFSA-finally-establishes-safe-sodium-and-chloride-values-after-ten-years). The EFSA 2016 potassium AI of 3500 mg/day was not verified in this session.

### Inferences: per-dinner (≈33% of a 2000 kcal day, ≈650 kcal) and per-week targets
| Metric | Daily | Per dinner (green) | Yellow | Red | Week (7 dinners) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salt | <5 g | ≤1.5 g | 1.5–2.5 g | >2.5 g | ≤10.5 g |
| Saturated fat | ≤10% E (≤22 g); ≤7% for LDL | ≤7 g (≤10% kcal) | 7–10 g | >10 g or >13% kcal | % kcal averaged |
| Trans fat | ≤1% E | ~0 | — | any industrial source | — |
| Free sugars | <10% E (50 g), ideally <5% | ≤5 g | 5–12 g | >12 g | ≤35–60 g |
| Fibre | ≥25–30 g; 14 g/1000 kcal | ≥9–10 g | 6–9 g | <6 g | ≥63–70 g |
| Fruit and vegetables | ≥400 g | ≥200 g veg | 100–200 g | <100 g | — |
| EPA+DHA | 250 mg (≈1750 mg/wk) | — | — | — | 1–2 oily fish portions/wk (ESC); ALA proxy if vegetarian |
| Potassium | 3.5 g | ≥1.2 g | — | — | — |
| Glycaemic load | — | ≤20 | 20–30 | >30 | — |

- The per-dinner share is a design choice. Dinner is commonly the largest meal (30–40%). The app should use the user's actual kcal target rather than a fixed 2000.
- Score mapping suggestion: each metric scores 0–10 by linear interpolation between the green bound (10) and the red bound (0). The goal score is a weighted mean of its metrics. Any red flag caps the meal at about 6. The weekly score averages meal scores and adds weekly-sum criteria (fish, legumes and salt total).
- Weights are an inference from the effect sizes above.
  - Heart: saturated fat 30%, fibre (and soluble fibre) 25%, salt 20%, unsaturated-fat sources/nuts 15%, trans fat flag 10%.
  - Glycaemia: GL 35%, fibre density 25%, sugars 20%, protein 10%, whole-grain share 10%.

## 4. UK FSA front-of-pack traffic lights (per 100 g) as product-level flags

### Takeaway
Per 100 g of food:

| Nutrient | Green | Amber | Red |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fat | ≤3 g | >3–20 g | >20 g |
| Saturates | ≤1.5 g | >1.5–5 g | >5 g |
| Sugars | ≤5 g | >5–15 g | >15 g |
| Salt | ≤0.3 g | >0.3–1.5 g | >1.5 g |

Red also applies when a single portion exceeds 30% of the reference intake (25% for a portion of food >100 g, which is the relevant rule for a dinner) [K].

### Cited Findings
- Per-100 g bands as above [F]. Source: [Milton Keynes Univ. Hospital NHS leaflet](https://www.mkuh.nhs.uk/document/understanding-food-labels), see also [Rowett/Aberdeen traffic light guide](https://abdn.ac.uk/rowett/documents/Traffic_light_guide.pdf)
- Portion-based red thresholds [K]:
  - Rule: >30% RI per portion, or >25% for portions over 100 g.
  - Reference intakes: fat 70 g, saturates 20 g, sugars 90 g (total sugars), salt 6 g.
  - Per-100 ml drink bands are halved.
  - Not confirmed from a fetched page. The primary source is the DHSC/FSA 2016 "Guide to creating a front of pack nutrition label".

### Inferences
- For a whole dinner (portion >100 g), red per portion means:
  - fat >17.5 g
  - saturates >5 g
  - sugars >22.5 g
  - salt >1.5 g
- This matches the per-dinner salt red/yellow boundary in section 3. Use FSA portion rules as the "red flag" layer and the stricter guideline-derived targets as the "green" layer.
- Per-100 g bands are best used to flag individual ingredients/products (e.g. cheese, processed meat analogues, sauces), not whole dinners.
