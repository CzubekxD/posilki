# Liver offloading (MASLD) and Mediterranean diet (MEDAS): measurable criteria for scoring dinners

## What do EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 and AASLD guidance say (alcohol, sugar/SSB, sat fat, UPF, fibre, omega-3, coffee, weight loss, Mediterranean)?

### Takeaway
The EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 guideline is mostly qualitative about food. It recommends a Mediterranean-like diet, limiting ultra-processed food (UPF), avoiding sugar-sweetened beverages (SSB), and avoiding or stopping alcohol. Its only hard numbers are weight-loss targets: ≥5% body weight to reduce liver fat, 7–10% to improve inflammation, ≥10% to improve fibrosis. It sets no g/day limits for fructose, saturated fat or fibre. An app therefore has to borrow those numbers from general dietary guidance or from the meta-analyses in the next section.

### Cited Findings
- EASL-EASD-EASO 2024, diet: "improving diet quality (similar to the Mediterranean dietary pattern)" together with "limiting the consumption of ultra-processed food" and "avoiding sugar-sweetened beverages" should be recommended to improve liver injury. LoE 2, strong recommendation, strong consensus. A separate statement says there is little evidence that better diet quality improves clinical liver outcomes (LoE 3). — [EASL-EASD-EASO CPG 2024, Diabetologia/J Hepatol co-publication, executive summary](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)
- EASL 2024, weight loss: aim for a sustained reduction of "≥5% to reduce liver fat, 7–10% to improve liver inflammation" and "≥10% to improve fibrosis" (LoE 2, strong). Diet- and behaviour-induced weight loss should be recommended (LoE 1, strong). — [same source](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)
- EASL 2024, alcohol: document intake in everyone; people with steatotic liver disease "should be discouraged from consuming alcohol"; all alcohol "should be stopped completely and permanently" in advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis (LoE 3, strong). — [same source](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)
- EASL 2024, coffee: a statement only, not a recommendation. Coffee is "associated with improvements in liver damage" in observational studies (LoE 4). — [same source](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)
- The executive summary contains no separate fructose, saturated-fat, fibre or omega-3 numeric recommendation. — [same source](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)
- Secondary summaries, used for orientation only:
  - Regular soda at >4 servings/week was associated with 45% higher MASLD risk (cited to EASL 2024).
  - Coffee benefit appears above about 3 cups/day; guidelines are said to suggest >2–3 cups/day.
  - 150 min/week of moderate activity plus resistance training 2–3×/week.
  - Source: [diabetesed.net review](https://diabetesed.net/updates-in-lifestyle-therapy-for-masld-and-mash)

### Inferences
- For a dinner score, the guideline-backed hard rules are:
  - alcohol: none = good; any = yellow; red flag if the user has advanced fibrosis;
  - SSB with the meal: red/yellow;
  - UPF share: lower is better;
  - Mediterranean-likeness: positive.
- Energy matters only at the week or person level, as a deficit supporting 5–10% weight loss. A per-dinner kcal cap derived from the user's target can be used as a proxy.
- Saturated fat, fibre and free sugar need generic thresholds. Typical choices are WHO free sugars <10% of energy, saturated fat <10% of energy and fibre ≥25–30 g/day. These come from my general knowledge and were not verified in this session; verify them before citing.

- I did not reach the EASL full-text tables (page numbers). Section-level citations come from the executive summary only.

## What is the evidence for fructose/SSB, red/processed meat, UPF and refined carbs in MASLD?

### Takeaway
SSB risk rises monotonically with intake. Even <1 cup/week carries about RR 1.14, and ≥7 cups/week about RR 1.53. Processed red meat adds about 11% to the odds per 25 g/day. Unprocessed red meat risk plateaus at about 25–30 g/day. UPF adds about 6% risk per 10% of the diet. All of this is observational and low-to-moderate quality evidence.

### Cited Findings
- SSB and NAFLD meta-analysis (12 studies, n=35,705):
  - Pooled RR 1.39 (1.29–1.50).
  - Non-linear monotonic dose-response: RR 1.10 at 1 cup/week and 1.56 at 7 cups/week, with 1 cup = 150–200 mL.
  - By band: <1 cup/week RR 1.14; 1–6 cups/week RR 1.26; ≥7 cups/week RR 1.53.
  - Caveats: mostly cross-sectional designs; possible publication bias; dose-response data came only from Chinese populations.
  - Source: [Chen et al., IJERPH 2019;16:2192](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/16/12/2192/htm)
- Red meat and NAFLD dose-response meta-analysis, highest vs lowest intake:
  - Processed red meat: OR 1.20 (1.04–1.37), linear, OR 1.11 per +25 g/day.
  - Unprocessed red meat: OR 1.28 (1.05–1.55). The relationship is non-linear: risk rises up to about 25 g/day (the Discussion says it peaks at 30 g/day, about +15%) and then plateaus.
  - Caveats: after trim-and-fill correction all estimates became non-significant; the authors rate the overall evidence quality as low.
  - Source: [PMC11046257, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11046257/)
- UPF and NAFLD meta-analysis (10 studies, n=513,440):
  - Highest vs lowest RR 1.22 (1.14–1.31).
  - RR 1.06 (1.04–1.09) per +10% UPF share.
  - Heterogeneity was high (I² ~78%).
  - Source: [Frontiers in Nutrition 2025, 10.3389/fnut.2025.1631975](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1631975)

### Inferences
Proposed per-dinner flags:
- **SSB/juice with dinner:** yellow at any serving; red when the week reaches ≥7 cups.
- **Processed meat:** yellow above 0 g; red at ≥50 g in one meal, which is 2×25 g increments.
- **Unprocessed red meat:** keep the weekly average ≤~25–30 g/day equivalent, i.e. ≤~175–210 g/week. Weekly over that = yellow.
- **UPF share of dinner energy:** <10% good, 10–30% yellow, >30% red. These are heuristic cut-points I chose, not taken from a source.
- **Fructose from whole fruit vs liquid:** no dose-specific evidence retrieved. Treat liquid fructose (SSB, juice) as the risk factor and whole fruit as neutral or positive. This is consistent with MEDAS counting fruit positively.

### Gaps
- I retrieved no fructose-specific g/day threshold (e.g. a "<50 g/day" type figure) from a primary source.
- Refined carbohydrates / glycaemic index in MASLD: not researched for lack of time; no cited numbers.

## The 14-item MEDAS: items, thresholds, and which are measurable at the dinner/week level

### Takeaway
MEDAS gives 1 point per item met, for 0–14 points in total. A score ≥9 is conventionally "good adherence" (PREDIMED convention, from my general knowledge, not verified here). At the dinner or week level the app can measure about 9 items: olive oil as the main fat, vegetables, legumes, fish, red/processed meat, butter/cream, white meat preference, sofrito and nuts. Wine is the conflicting item: MEDAS rewards it, while the liver guidance discourages alcohol.

### Cited Findings
The 14 items in the PREDIMED-Plus version, 1 point each — [OUP table](https://academic.oup.com/view-large/133887767):

| # | Item | Threshold for 1 point | Measurable from dinners? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Olive oil is the main culinary fat | yes | Per dinner: fat type |
| 2 | Olive oil per day | ≥4 tbsp | Partly: share of the day |
| 3 | Vegetables per day | ≥2 servings, ≥1 raw or salad | Per dinner: ≥1 serving |
| 4 | Fruit per day (natural juice included) | ≥3 | Mostly outside dinner |
| 5 | Red meat, hamburger or meat products per day | <1 serving | Week: count |
| 6 | Butter, margarine or cream per day | <1 serving (12 g) | Per dinner |
| 7 | Sweetened/carbonated drinks per day | <1 | Per dinner |
| 8 | Wine per week | ≥7 glasses | Conflicts with liver guidance |
| 9 | Legumes per week | ≥3 servings (150 g) | Week: count |
| 10 | Fish/shellfish per week | ≥3 servings | Week: count |
| 11 | Commercial sweets/pastries per week | <2 | Week |
| 12 | Nuts per week | ≥3 servings (30 g) | Week: count |
| 13 | Prefers chicken, turkey or rabbit over red meat | yes | Week: ratio |
| 14 | Dishes with sofrito per week | ≥2 | Week: count |

- The commercial-sweets threshold differs between versions. A PREDIMED goal table lists <3 servings/week for commercial bakery goods, sweets and pastries; the PREDIMED-Plus item above uses <2. That table also lists PREDIMED fruit ≥3/day, vegetables ≥3/day, nuts, fish and legumes ≥3/week, sofrito ≥2/week, soda <1/day and red meat <1/day. — [BMC Public Health 2016, Table 1](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12889-016-3370-9/tables/1)
- Note that the BMC table shows PREDIMED goals, not MEDAS scoring, and lists vegetables as ≥3/day. The MEDAS item is ≥2/day. — [same](https://bmcpublichealth.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12889-016-3370-9/tables/1)

### Inferences
- For a vegetarian user (from the project context), items 5 and 13 are satisfied by default or should be treated as N/A. Item 10 (fish) cannot be met by a vegetarian. One option is to give legumes + nuts + ALA sources partial credit and re-normalise the score over the applicable items.
- For liver scoring, override item 8: alcohol scores 0, or the item is removed.
- Weekly dinner targets derived from MEDAS (7 dinners):
  - legumes ≥3 dinners;
  - fish ≥3 dinners, or N/A for vegetarians;
  - sofrito or tomato-onion-garlic-olive oil base ≥2 dinners;
  - nuts ≥3 servings across the week;
  - every dinner contains ≥1 vegetable serving and uses olive oil as its fat.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the original Schröder et al. 2011 (J Nutr 141:1140) text. Exact original wording, and the ≥9 vs ≥10 cut-off for "high adherence", should be verified.

## Proposed component weights for a "liver" score; validated indices used in NAFLD trials

### Takeaway
I found no validated, NAFLD-specific diet index with official weights. Trials mostly use MEDAS, or MedDiet scores more generally, as the adherence measure. One cross-sectional study (PubMed 24064253) reported that MedDiet adherence was associated with NAFLD severity. A liver score therefore has to be a designed heuristic built on guideline priorities.

### Cited Findings
- Adherence to the Mediterranean diet has been associated with NAFLD severity. — [PubMed 24064253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24064253/). I did not read the full text; this claim is based on the title only.
- The guideline hierarchy behind the weights is: weight loss (LoE 1, strong) > Mediterranean pattern / avoid UPF / avoid SSB (LoE 2, strong) > alcohol (LoE 3, strong) > coffee (LoE 4, statement only). — [EASL 2024](https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/0a880a86-ba08-4c65-b360-8e3c6f92a121/s00125-024-06196-3.pdf)

### Inferences
Suggested 1–10 dinner liver score. These weights are proposed, not validated.
- **Energy within the user's dinner target (deficit-supporting): 25%.** Over by >20% = yellow; over by >50% = red.
- **Mediterranean components: 25%.** Olive oil as the fat, ≥1 vegetable serving (≥150–200 g), legumes, wholegrain.
- **Sugar: 15%.** No SSB/juice, and added sugar <~10 g in the meal (heuristic: about a third of a 10%-energy daily limit at ~2000 kcal, or ~17 g). Any SSB = red.
- **UPF / processed meat: 15%.** Processed meat >0 = yellow; ≥50 g = red; UPF >30% of energy = red.
- **Saturated fat: 10%.** <10% of meal energy good; >15% yellow (heuristic).
- **Fibre: 10%.** ≥8–10 g per dinner, i.e. a third of 25–30 g/day (heuristic).
- **Hard caps:** any alcohol caps the score at ≤6 (yellow). With advanced fibrosis, alcohol is contraindicated (red, score ≤3).
- **Weekly score:** average of the dinner scores, plus MEDAS-derived weekly bonuses: legumes ≥3, fish ≥3 or N/A, nuts ≥3, sofrito ≥2, red meat ≤~200 g/week, processed meat ≈0.

### Gaps
- I found no validated weights for HEI or DASH in NAFLD trials in this session.
- PREDIMED-Plus (energy-reduced MedDiet) liver-fat outcomes were not retrieved.
- The CENTRAL / Green-MED trials (Gepner, Yaskolka Meir) were not retrieved: polyphenol- and green-plant-based variants, said to reduce intrahepatic fat.
